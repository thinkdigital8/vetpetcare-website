Best Cat Litter for Kittens: A Complete Guide

By VetPet Care Updated 11 Sep 2026 7 min read

Not every litter that's fine for an adult cat is fine for a kitten. Young kittens explore the world mouth-first, and a litter that's perfectly safe for a grown cat can be a real hazard for a curious 8-week-old. Here's how to choose the right litter at every stage, and when it's safe to switch to a standard clumping bentonite formula.

Quick answer: Kittens under 8-12 weeks should use a non-clumping, low-dust litter such as recycled paper or plain clay pellets, since swallowed clumping clay can swell inside the gut. Once a kitten is weaned, eating solid food and no longer mouthing litter — usually from around 3 months — you can transition to a quality clumping bentonite litter like VetPet Care's.

Why kitten litter needs special care

Kittens investigate everything with their mouths, including their litter box. A tiny amount of ingested litter isn't unusual while they're learning, but the material matters a great deal:

  • Clumping bentonite clay is designed to swell on contact with moisture. That's exactly what makes it excellent for adult litter boxes, and exactly why it's risky if a young kitten swallows a meaningful amount — it can swell inside the digestive tract.
  • Dust is more of a concern for kittens than adult cats, since their respiratory systems are still developing. Look for low-dust or dust-free formulas at every stage.
  • Texture that's too coarse can be uncomfortable for small paws, which can put a kitten off using the box entirely.

When to switch to clumping litter

Most kittens are ready to move onto clumping bentonite litter from around 3 months old, once they're weaned onto solid food and have largely grown out of chewing or licking their litter. Make the switch gradually:

  • Mix a small amount of the new clumping litter into the existing box.
  • Increase the ratio over 7-10 days, watching for consistent, comfortable use.
  • Keep an eye out for any mouthing behaviour returning — if it does, slow the transition down.
  • Once fully switched, a 99% dust-free bentonite formula gives the best combination of odour control and easy scooping going forward.

Choosing the right box size

A kitten-appropriate box makes litter training far easier:

  • Low sides. A kitten should be able to step in and out without climbing — roughly half the height of an adult box.
  • Shallow fill. 1-2 inches (3-5 cm) of litter is enough for a kitten; adult depths of 4 inches can feel overwhelming.
  • Multiple locations. Keep a box on every floor of the home a kitten has access to, so accidents from not finding the box in time are less likely.
  • Upgrade as they grow. Move to a standard adult-size box by around 4-6 months, or sooner if the kitten is clearly outgrowing the starter box.

Tips for smooth litter training

  • Show them the box. After meals and naps, gently place the kitten in the litter box so they learn to associate it with the right moment.
  • Keep it clean. Kittens are put off by a dirty box just as much as adult cats — scoop daily from the start.
  • Avoid sudden litter changes. Once a kitten is comfortable with a formula, switch brands gradually to avoid box-avoidance.
  • Watch, don't force. Never physically push a kitten's paws into the litter — let them dig and explore on their own.

Frequently asked questions

Is clumping cat litter safe for kittens?
Clumping bentonite litter is not recommended for kittens under about 8-12 weeks old, because young kittens explore with their mouths and swallowed clumping clay can swell inside the digestive tract. Use a non-clumping litter until they are older and past the mouthing stage.
What litter should I use for a kitten?
For kittens under 8-12 weeks, use a non-clumping, low-dust litter such as recycled paper or plain clay pellets. Once the kitten is older, eating solid food reliably and no longer mouthing litter, you can transition to a quality clumping bentonite formula.
When can I switch my kitten to clumping litter?
Most kittens can move to clumping bentonite litter from around 3 months old, once they are fully weaned and have stopped putting litter in their mouths. Introduce it gradually by mixing it with their current litter over a week or two.
What size litter box does a kitten need?
Choose a shallow box with low sides, roughly half the size of an adult box, so a kitten can climb in and out easily. Upgrade to a standard adult-size box as the kitten grows, usually by 4-6 months.
