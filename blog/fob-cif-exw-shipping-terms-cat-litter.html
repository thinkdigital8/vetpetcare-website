FOB vs CIF vs EXW: Shipping Terms Every Cat Litter Importer Should Know

By VetPet Care Published 16 Oct 2026 7 min read

Every export quote eventually comes with three letters attached — FOB, CIF, or occasionally EXW — and those letters quietly decide who pays for what, who carries the risk, and where your responsibility as a buyer actually begins. Misreading them is one of the most common ways a "great price" turns into a higher landed cost than expected.

Quick answer: EXW (Ex Works) means the buyer handles everything from the factory door onward. FOB (Free on Board) means the seller gets the goods loaded onto the vessel at the origin port, and the buyer takes over from there. CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight) means the seller arranges and pays for freight and insurance all the way to the destination port. Each shifts cost and risk to a different point in the journey.

What Incoterms are and why they matter

Incoterms (International Commercial Terms) are a standardized set of trade terms, published and maintained by the International Chamber of Commerce, that define exactly where the seller's responsibility ends and the buyer's begins in an international shipment. They cover three things in particular: who pays for transport at each leg, who arranges insurance, and at what point risk of loss or damage transfers from seller to buyer.

Getting the term right matters because it changes your actual landed cost calculation — a lower quoted unit price under one term can end up costing more once you add the freight, insurance, and customs responsibilities it doesn't include.

EXW: Ex Works

Under EXW, the seller's obligation ends the moment the goods are made available at their factory or warehouse. Everything from that point — loading onto a truck, inland transport to the port, export customs clearance, ocean freight, insurance, import clearance, and final delivery — is the buyer's responsibility to arrange and pay for.

EXW typically produces the lowest quoted unit price, since it excludes the most cost. It suits experienced importers who already have a freight forwarder and customs broker relationship in the exporting country, since arranging origin-side logistics without local presence can be inefficient and sometimes more expensive than letting the seller handle it.

FOB: Free on Board

Under FOB, the seller handles inland transport from the factory to the port, export customs clearance, and loading the goods onto the shipping vessel. Once the goods are on board at the origin port, responsibility and risk transfer to the buyer, who then arranges and pays for ocean freight, marine insurance, and everything from the destination port onward.

FOB is the most commonly used term in cat litter export, since it gives the buyer control over choosing their own freight forwarder and carrier — useful for buyers who import from multiple countries and want to consolidate freight arrangements or negotiate shipping rates directly.

CIF: Cost, Insurance, and Freight

Under CIF, the seller does everything FOB covers, plus arranges and pays for ocean freight and marine insurance all the way to the named destination port. The buyer's responsibility begins once the goods arrive at that port — import customs clearance, duties, and inland delivery from there are on the buyer.

CIF is often the most convenient starting point for first-time importers, since it bundles freight and insurance into a single quoted price and removes the need to independently source a freight forwarder for that leg of the journey.

Incoterms rules are periodically updated by the International Chamber of Commerce (most recently Incoterms 2020), and exact obligations can vary with the specific contract terms agreed between buyer and seller. Always confirm the applicable Incoterms version and exact responsibilities in writing with your supplier and customs broker before finalizing an order.

Side-by-side comparison

In short: EXW shifts the most work and cost to the buyer and typically shows the lowest quoted price; FOB splits responsibility roughly at the origin port and is the most widely used for ocean freight; CIF shifts the most work to the seller up to the destination port and typically shows the highest quoted price, since freight and insurance are already included.

Which term should you choose?

For a first order, or for an importer without an established freight forwarder relationship, CIF usually removes the most friction — one quoted price covers the goods to your port, and your logistics responsibilities start only from clearance onward. For repeat buyers or larger-volume importers who want more control over freight costs and can negotiate better rates through their own forwarder, FOB often works out more cost-efficient over time. EXW suits buyers who already run origin-side logistics for other product lines and can fold a new supplier into existing arrangements with minimal extra cost.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between FOB and CIF?
Under FOB (Free on Board), the seller's responsibility ends once the goods are loaded onto the shipping vessel at the origin port, and the buyer arranges and pays for ocean freight and insurance from there. Under CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight), the seller arranges and pays for freight and insurance all the way to the destination port, bundling that cost into the quoted price.
Is EXW cheaper than FOB?
The quoted EXW price is typically the lowest of the three because it excludes all transport, but that's not the same as the cheapest total landed cost. EXW shifts origin-side costs like inland trucking and export clearance onto the buyer, which can be inefficient or expensive to arrange without a local presence or freight forwarder in the exporting country.
Which Incoterm should a first-time importer choose?
CIF is usually the most straightforward starting point for a first-time importer, since the seller handles freight and insurance to the destination port and the buyer's logistics responsibilities start only from port clearance onward. FOB suits importers who already have a reliable freight forwarder and want more control over freight costs and carrier choice.
