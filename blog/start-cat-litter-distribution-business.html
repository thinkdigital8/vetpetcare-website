How to Start a Cat Litter Distribution Business: A Step-by-Step Guide

By VetPet Care Published 26 Sep 2026 9 min read

Cat litter is a repeat-purchase product: every cat household buys it again and again, which is why it makes a solid anchor product for a pet distribution business. The catch is that it is heavy, freight-sensitive and brand-loyal, so the difference between a profitable distributor and a stuck one usually comes down to decisions made before the first order. Here is a practical path from idea to first container.

Quick answer: Research your market and shelf prices, choose a direct manufacturer with export experience, calculate your landed cost per bag, test samples for clumping and dust, then place a right-sized first order and reorder once sell-through is proven. Start with the pack size your channel needs (5kg for shops and e-commerce, 25kg for wholesale) and confirm MOQ, FOB and CIF terms in writing.

Step 1: Research your market

Before contacting any supplier, spend a week in the market you plan to sell into. Visit pet shops and supermarkets, browse online marketplaces, and note what is actually on the shelf:

  • Price points. Record shelf prices per kilogram across budget, mid-range and premium brands so you know where a new product can sit.
  • Litter types. Note the mix of clumping bentonite, non-clumping clay, silica and plant-based litters. A market dominated by non-clumping clay may be an opening for a quality clumping product; our clumping vs non-clumping guide covers how buyers compare them.
  • Pack sizes and fragrances. Which sizes sell fastest, and are scented or unscented variants missing?
  • Sales channels. Independent pet shops, supermarket chains, veterinary clinics, catteries and online marketplaces each buy differently and expect different pack sizes and terms.

The goal is a one-page picture: who you will sell to, at what shelf price, in which pack size. Every later decision hangs off it.

Step 2: Choose the right supplier

Supplier choice is the decision that is hardest to reverse. Prioritize a direct manufacturer over a trading company: you get factory-direct pricing, consistent quality and someone accountable for the product. Confirm the basics before you go further:

  • Export track record and the countries already served
  • Retail (5kg) and bulk (25kg) pack sizes, and available fragrances
  • Private label or custom branding if you want your own brand, explained in our private label guide
  • Export documentation, including a Certificate of Analysis and Safety Data Sheet (VetPet Care shares both on the certificates page)
  • Supply consistency for reorders, not just the first shipment

For a fuller checklist, see our guide to sourcing cat litter from India.

The supplier's price is only one line of your real cost. Build a simple per-bag landed cost so you can see your true margin before you commit:

Then work backwards from the shelf price you found in Step 1: subtract the retailer's margin to see the price you can charge, and compare it with your landed cost. Container capacity matters here because it sets how many bags share the freight bill; our container loading guide explains why weight, not volume, is usually the limit for cat litter.

Costs, duty rates and freight rates vary by route, season and destination. Treat this table as a framework and rely on written quotes from your supplier, forwarder and customs broker for actual numbers.

Step 4: Test samples before you commit

Never order a container on a spec sheet alone. Request samples in the fragrances you plan to sell and test them the way your customers will:

  • Clumping strength. Do clumps hold together when scooped, or crumble?
  • Dust. Pour a bag and watch for a cloud. Low dust is a strong selling point; see what "dust-free" really means.
  • Odor control after 24 hours and after several days of use.
  • Packaging. Are the bags tough enough for freight, and is the shelf presentation good?
  • Real cats. Put it in a few litter boxes and see whether cats accept it.

Step 5: Place your first order and grow

Confirm in writing the MOQ, pack size mix, FOB and CIF price, production lead time, payment terms and documentation. A sensible pattern is to start with the pack size your main channel needs, prove sell-through, then reorder and widen the range: add a second fragrance or the 25kg bulk bag, or approach a new region. As volume grows, private label lets you build your own brand on the same product.

Track sell-through per outlet in your first two months. Reorder timing matters as much as the first order, because running out of stock is the fastest way to lose a shelf slot to a competitor.

Common mistakes new distributors make

  • Judging by ex-factory price alone and ignoring freight, duty and clearance.
  • Skipping samples and discovering a quality problem after a container has shipped.
  • Ordering too many fragrances at once instead of proving one or two first.
  • Ignoring documentation until the container is at port.
  • Working with a trading company that cannot answer production or quality questions.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start a cat litter distribution business?
Research demand and pricing in your target market, choose a direct manufacturer with export experience, calculate your landed cost and margin, test samples for clumping and dust, then place a first container order and reorder as sell-through is proven.
Do I need a large investment to become a cat litter distributor?
It depends on your order size. A full container is the most cost-efficient way to ship, but many distributors start with a smaller trial order to validate demand. Exact minimum order quantities and pricing are confirmed by the manufacturer once you share your destination and target volume.
Should I sell 5kg retail bags or 25kg bulk bags?
5kg bags suit pet shops, supermarkets and e-commerce shelves, while 25kg bags suit wholesale, catteries and freight efficiency. Many distributors start with 5kg for retail sell-through and add 25kg once repeat demand is established.
How do I calculate my margin on imported cat litter?
Add the supplier price, freight and insurance, import duty and taxes, port and clearance charges, and inland delivery to get your landed cost per bag, then compare it with the price your customers will pay after any retailer margin. Ask the supplier for both FOB and CIF pricing to make this comparison accurate.
