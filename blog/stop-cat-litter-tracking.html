How to Stop Cat Litter Tracking Through Your House

By VetPet Care Updated 1 Sep 2026 6 min read

Finding gritty granules on the hallway floor, the sofa, or even the kitchen counter is one of the most common complaints from cat owners. Litter tracking is almost never about a "messy" cat - it's about box setup, mat placement, and the litter itself. Here's what actually works.

Quick answer: Most tracking is fixed with three changes: a textured trapping mat right at the box exit, a higher-sided or covered box to contain digging, and a dust-free, uniform-granule clumping litter that doesn't crumble underfoot.

  2. Mat placement that actually catches litter

Why cats track litter out of the box

When a cat digs to bury waste, loose granules and dust lodge between the fur and pads of their paws. As they walk away from the box, those particles drop off - usually within the first few steps, but sometimes much further if the cat jumps onto furniture right after using the box. Three factors make this worse:

  • Fine or dusty litter clings to fur more easily than larger, drier granules.
  • Low-sided or open boxes let more litter get kicked or carried straight over the edge.
  • No transition zone at the box exit means the first tracked granules land directly on bare floor or carpet.

Mat placement that actually catches litter

A trapping mat only works if it's positioned where paws actually land:

  • Place the mat directly against the box entrance - not a few inches away - so the cat has to step on it leaving the box.
  • Choose a honeycomb or ridged texture over a flat mat; the raised cells pull granules off paw pads instead of letting them slide across.
  • Size up. A mat roughly 60cm x 90cm gives 2-3 full paw cycles before the cat reaches open floor.
  • Shake the mat out over the litter box daily so trapped granules go back where they belong instead of building up.

Whichever style you choose, keep the box on a hard floor rather than carpet - it makes any stray granules easy to spot and sweep up before they spread.

Why the litter itself matters

Granule size, weight and dust level all affect how much litter leaves the box on a cat's paws:

  • Larger, uniform clumping granules are heavier and less likely to wedge between paw pads than fine or powdery litter.
  • Low-dust formulas mean less fine residue gets carried out as a visible dust film on floors and furniture.
  • Consistent granule shape reduces the crumbly fragments that are the main source of tracking. VetPet Care's 99% dust-free bentonite uses rounded, uniform granules for exactly this reason - alongside gentler contact for sensitive paws.

Quick fix checklist

  • ✔ Place a ridged trapping mat directly at the box exit
  • ✔ Switch to a high-sided, covered, or top-entry box if kick-out is heavy
  • ✔ Fill to the recommended depth - not more
  • ✔ Use a dust-free, uniform-granule clumping litter
  • ✔ Vacuum or sweep the mat and surrounding floor daily
  • ✔ Position the box on hard flooring, away from carpeted walkways

Frequently asked questions

Why does my cat track litter everywhere?
Litter sticks to the fur between a cat's paw pads after digging or covering waste, then falls off with each step outside the box. Smaller or dustier granules and rougher paw fur make tracking worse.
Do litter mats actually reduce tracking?
Yes. A textured trapping mat placed directly in front of and around the litter box catches loose granules from the cat's paws before they reach the rest of the floor, cutting visible tracking significantly.
Does litter granule size affect tracking?
Yes. Larger, heavier clumping granules are less likely to lodge between paw pads and fall off closer to the box, while fine or dusty litter travels further and tracks more.
Should I switch litter types to stop tracking?
Not usually necessary. A quality clumping bentonite litter with dust-free, uniform granules combined with a trapping mat and a covered or high-sided box solves most tracking issues without changing litter type.
