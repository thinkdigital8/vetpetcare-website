Buying Guide

Clumping vs. Non-Clumping Cat Litter: What Bulk Buyers Should Know

Choosing the right litter type affects freight weight, shelf turnover, and customer repeat rate. Here's how clumping bentonite compares for importers and retailers.

Read the full guide

Clumping bentonite litter forms solid clumps around waste on contact, letting owners scoop only the soiled portion instead of replacing the entire tray. For distributors, that translates into a slower-depleting bag and stronger perceived value versus non-clumping silica or clay alternatives. Non-clumping litter is typically lighter to ship but requires full changes more often, which can hurt long-term customer retention. For import partners evaluating SKUs, clumping bentonite in 5kg retail and 25kg bulk formats - like VetPet Care's range - tends to perform best across both price-sensitive and premium shelf placements, since one formulation serves multiple price points through pack size alone.

Product Care

How Often Should You Really Change Cat Litter?

Daily scooping keeps odor down, but full litter box changes follow a different rhythm. We break down the maintenance schedule that keeps cats happiest.

Read the full guide

Most clumping litter, including VetPet Care's bentonite formula, is designed for daily scooping and a full replacement every 3 to 4 weeks per cat using the box. Multi-cat households or smaller trays may need a full change closer to every 2 weeks, since waste concentration accelerates odor buildup regardless of scent variant. Topping up litter to the recommended 4-inch depth between full changes maintains clumping strength and helps the fragrance layer - whether Lavender, Jasmine, or Non-Scented - stay effective for longer between replacements.

Export & Trade

Cat Litter Container Loading Guide: 20ft vs 40ft Capacity

Planning a full container order? Here's how many bags of cat litter actually fit in a 20ft or 40ft container, and why weight limits matter more than volume.

Read the full guide

Bentonite cat litter is dense enough that a container usually hits its legal road-weight limit before its internal volume is full, which means a 20ft container typically carries a similar tonnage to a 40ft container for a pure cat litter shipment — roughly 1,000 to 1,100 bags of 25kg litter, or around 26-28 tons. That makes 20ft the more cost-efficient booking for single-product orders, while 40ft only pays off when combined with lighter, bulkier cargo. VetPet Care plans exact loading and FOB/CIF pricing once your pack size mix and destination port are confirmed.

Cat Care Tips

Best Cat Litter for Multi-Cat Households

Odor compounds fast and boxes fill up faster with multiple cats. Here's how many boxes you need, which litter holds up, and how to stop shared-box conflicts.

Read the full guide

The standard rule for multi-cat homes is one litter box per cat, plus one extra, spread across separate low-traffic locations rather than lined up in a single room. Litter choice matters more under heavier daily use: clumping bentonite outperforms non-clumping clay and silica crystal because it isolates waste into removable clumps, letting a box stay usable between full changes even with two or three cats sharing the home. Scooping every box twice daily and doing a full change every 1-2 weeks - rather than the 3-4 week single-cat schedule - prevents the odor and avoidance cycle that leads to litter box problems in multi-cat households.

Buying Guide

Silica Gel vs Bentonite Cat Litter: Which Is Actually Better?

Crystal or clumping clay? Here's a balanced, head-to-head look at odor control, dust, cost per use and environmental impact to help you choose.

Read the full guide

Silica gel litter absorbs moisture into crystal granules rather than clumping, so owners typically rake out solids daily and follow a fixed full-change schedule - often at a higher price per bag. Bentonite clay swells and binds into a firm clump around waste, letting you scoop just the soiled portion daily and replace the litter every few weeks, usually at a lower price per bag. Neither is universally better: bentonite tends to suit multi-cat households and cost-conscious buyers, while silica gel appeals to single-cat owners who prefer fewer full changes.
